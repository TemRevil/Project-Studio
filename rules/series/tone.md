## WHO IS SPEAKING

A self-taught developer who learned by building real things, not by reading textbooks.
Someone who respects the viewer's intelligence and refuses to waste their time.
A person who finds tech genuinely fascinating but is also aware of how absurd it can be.
Direct. Confident. Occasionally funny in a way that doesn't try too hard.
Never performative. Never hype. Never fake enthusiasm.

The voice sounds like someone who knows the answer, chose to explain it clearly, and snuck in one sharp observation that makes you think.

## TONE PILLARS

### 1. DIRECTNESS
- Say the thing. Then stop.
- No preamble. No "great question."
- No building up to the point — start at the point.
- ✅ "LLMs hallucinate. RAG fixes that."
- ❌ "So today we're going to talk about a really interesting topic called RAG and why it matters..."

### 2. INTELLIGENCE RESPECT
- Never explain what the viewer already knows
- Never define basic terms unless the video is explicitly for beginners
- Never repeat the same idea in different words just to fill time
- Trust that they're watching because they want to learn, not because they need hand-holding

### 3. DRY SARCASM (when enabled)
- Used once per video. Maximum.
- Always directed at the technology or the situation, never at the viewer
- Deadpan delivery. No winking. No "get it?"
- The joke is in the observation, not in the performance
- ✅ "Turns out training on the entire internet doesn't mean you know what happened last Tuesday."
- ✅ "The model is very confident. The model is also very wrong. These two things coexist peacefully."
- ❌ "Haha just kidding guys!" — never, ever
- ❌ Sarcasm about the viewer's knowledge level — banned permanently

### 4. EARNED ENDINGS
- The last line is the whole point of the video
- It should feel like a door closing, not trailing off
- It can be a lesson, a reframe, a punch, or a quiet truth
- ✅ "So RAG didn't make the model smarter. It just gave it a library card."
- ❌ "And that's basically how RAG works! Hope you found this helpful!"

### 5. ZERO FILLER
Permanently banned words and phrases:
- "Basically" as a sentence opener
- "Let's dive in"
- "In today's video"
- "Stay tuned"
- "Hit that subscribe button"
- "Without further ado"
- "Super" as an adjective intensifier
- "Amazing" for anything that isn't actually amazing
- "Game changer"
- "Revolutionary"
- Any sentence that starts with "So,"

---

## LANGUAGE RULES

- Default: English
- Technical terms: always English, even in Arabic context
- Sentence length: 6–10 words for impact. Up to 14 for explanation. Never more.
- Rhythm matters. Vary sentence length intentionally.
- Short sentence after a complex one = breath = clarity.

---

## EMOTION RANGE

The voice is allowed to be:
- Curious
- Dry
- Confident
- Slightly amused
- Quietly impressed by something elegant
- Mildly exasperated by something absurd in tech

The voice is never:
- Excited in an artificial way
- Motivational-speaker energy
- Angry or frustrated at the viewer
- Overly casual ("yo guys what's up")
- Overly formal ("in this video, I shall endeavor to explain...")

---

## SARCASM CALIBRATION SCALE

Reference this before writing any sarcasm line:

```
Level 1 — Gentle observation    → "Which is a very polite way of saying it guessed."
Level 2 — Dry irony             → "The model is, technically, never wrong. It's just frequently fictional."
Level 3 — Sharp but fair        → "We trained it on everything humans ever wrote. And then we were surprised it inherited our confidence."
Level 4 — Too far (never use)   → Anything that punches at the viewer or a group of people
```

Default: Level 1–2. Level 3 only on special topics. Level 4 is banned.

## THE TEMY FILTER

Before finalizing any script, run it through this filter:

- Would temy actually say this out loud?
- Does it sound like a person or a content creator?
- Is there a single word that feels forced or unnatural?
- Is the sarcasm landing without needing to be explained?
- Does the ending feel earned or does it just stop?

If any answer is no — rewrite that line.
